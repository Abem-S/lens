# Muna Lens

A tutor that watches a student solve STEM problems on paper. A camera sees the page; Lens reads each handwritten step, finds the **first real mistake**, outlines it, and asks the student one Socratic question by voice. It never gives the answer.

Built for every student who studies on paper.

## Hard rules

1. **Secrets stay in `.env`** (gitignored). Commit only `.env.example` with variable names.
2. **Meta models do the intelligence**: seeing, reading, checking, talking, pointing, listening. Non-Meta parts are limited to voice output (Kokoro) and plumbing.
3. **A claimed mistake is a verified mistake.** When a step is algebra or calculus, SymPy must confirm the error before the tutor mentions it. A false alarm costs more than a missed error.
4. **Hints carry no answer.** A hint asks a question or points at a place; it never states the corrected step or the result.

Muse Spark Open gotchas:
- It is a reasoning model. Reasoning tokens count against `max_tokens`; with `max_tokens=300` it returned `None`. Use ≥1500 for replies, ≥3000 for page checks.
- It accepts audio (`{"type":"audio_url","audio_url":{"url":"data:audio/wav;base64,..."}}` and `input_audio` both work) and transcribes accurately, but a reply from audio starts after 4–5 s. Live speech goes through Omnilingual ASR → text instead.
- At low reasoning effort a hint leaked the step ("subtract 5 from one side…"). The guardrail (A4) is required, not optional.

Spark on Baseten stays the default. Re-test on 2026-10-30, 13 page reads over 11 minutes (`low` effort): 12 took 6.7–10.8 s (p90 10.8 s), and one queued 76 s before its first byte (81 s in total). Text replies took 1.4–1.7 s (the first, 3.3 s). The rule was "usable if p90 ≤ 15 s and no call over 45 s", so it failed on that one outlier. Glimmer is the self-hosted fallback. Reading quality is about even, and Glimmer never queues, but it is slower on text and costs GPU time while warm.

Muse Glimmer gotchas:
- On a 48 GB L40S, the FP8 weights plus the draft head take 37.3 GiB. At 32k context there was too little room left for the KV cache (0.52 GiB) and the engine failed to start. The app uses 16k context, `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.95` and 4 sequences, which leaves 1.87 GiB.
- vLLM prints its CLI args, so `--api-key` would put the key in the Modal logs. The key goes in through `VLLM_API_KEY` instead.
- The reasoning parser (`muse_glimmer`) sends reasoning into `reasoning_content`, so `content` holds only the answer and `response_format=json_object` works.

## Product decisions (settled)

- **Web app only.** Works with a **laptop webcam** (student holds the notebook up and points: "this line?") and a **phone camera** paired by QR code (phone over the notebook; laptop shows the screen and plays voice). Phone runs in the mobile browser; no native app. Meta glasses appear only on the vision slide (Ethiopia is not a supported country for the Wearables toolkit).
- **Proactive tutor.** It learns what the student is working on by asking and conversing, and confirms rather than assumes. It keeps private notes on the problem for the session (`lens/notes.py`: the problem with every given, what is asked, a short teaching plan, the student's approach, where they are), rewritten by a small Spark call after a student turn of three or more words and after a check that read new steps, and put into every reply, so the givens survive a long talk (the conversation itself keeps the last 20 turns); "next question" clears them, a page read differently does not. It speaks up by itself, never over the student: a short "good" for a correct new step (at most every 12 s; a warmer word for one that took 25 s or more, at most every 150 s), a check-in after 20 s at the page with no ink or talk and one more concrete one 45 s later (at most two per pause, not within 30 s of a hint, not when muted), a question about where the work is heading when the notes say the approach cannot reach what is asked (never called a mistake; at most every 120 s and once per stage), a remark when a step is crossed out, and one look at the page when it changed but no step was read, e.g. a diagram (at most every 120 s). The conversation model is told the live state (writing / paused / away from tracking, else the pixel detector; time since the last new step; steps read). Testing showed the tutor lost problem context over long sessions; resolved by adding persistent session state in lens/notes.py.
- **All STEM subjects.** No subject-specific builds; light prompt tuning only where testing demands it.
- **Live, interruptible voice conversation.** English only.
- **Minimal Lens screen**, designed fresh: live page, outline on the mistake, tutor pointer, the hint and captions. No list of the steps read: user testing found on-screen step transcriptions showed half-read steps and added clutter; the screen now displays only the error outline and tutor pointer; "Read my page aloud" stays.
- **Live tracking, proactive checks, no button.** The camera is a live feed and SAM 3.1 tracks the work as it is written (the core design); Lens checks a line by itself when it is finished and the hand is clear. There is no "check my work" button. Muse Spark still reads a finished line from one good frame, since it cannot process video. The in-browser pixel detector stays as the fallback when tracking is unavailable; in webcam mode it skips the skin test (the student's face and hands are always in view). Cadence and cost are measured in testing.
- **The tutor sees, without nagging.** While the student talks the browser sends a camera picture every 0.5 s (768 px, never queued behind a busy socket) and for 1.5 s after (`lib/look.ts`). A turn about what the camera shows ("can you see…", "what numbers am I showing", "how many fingers", "watch this", "what am I doing"), or one when nothing has been read yet, gets up to 6 of them, spread over the turn, in order and labelled; the server waits up to 0.6 s for a picture after the turn ends, 2.5 s after "watch". Measured 2026-11-03 (Spark, Baseten): first token 1.7 s with no picture, 6.5 s with one, 6.5 s with four, 6.8 s with six; with six it named a 2, 4, 6 sequence in order. So pictures go only with such turns: "Is this right?" is answered from the page already read, with pointing. A filler ("Let me look.") is spoken if the reply has not started after 1.2 s, at most once a minute.
- **Say "step", never "line".** Lens refers to the student's work by what they wrote ("where you moved the five across") or as a step; a filter rewrites any "line 2" a model still produces. The page shows only the outline on the mistake and the tutor's pointer, with no ticks over the writing.
- **Phone video is peer to peer.** The phone streams its camera to the laptop over WebRTC (signalled over the pairing socket); on the same Wi-Fi it never leaves the room. The JPEG relay through the server is only the fallback, and it never queues frames.
- **Standalone architecture.** Lens runs as an independent, self-contained service.
- **Hosting:** during the build the app runs on Modal (`make deploy-web`, real HTTPS, so a phone pairs from any network); The web application and GPU inference services run on **Modal** (`make deploy-web` for frontend/backend with automatic HTTPS, `make deploy-gpu` for SAM and voice). Tests and evals run on Modal too (`make test-modal`, `make eval-modal`) for fast cloud execution without requiring local GPU/RAM.
- **GPU runs only while someone uses Lens.** No Modal app pins a container up (no `min_containers`, no `update_autoscaler`); each scales to zero on its own `scaledown_window` (SAM 3.1 60 s, voice and still-frame SAM 90 s, Glimmer 180 s; the web app is CPU only, 300 s). The browser decides presence (`lib/presence.ts`): Lens rests after `LENS_IDLE_MIN` (5 min) with no camera movement, speech or tap, at once when the tab is hidden, and after 4× that with no tap, speech or check even if a webcam keeps seeing movement. Resting closes live tracking and stops the presence heartbeat; the server (`lens/warm.py`) pings voice and SAM only on fresh heartbeats (voice only once it is on, or in the first 3 min), SAM 3.1 only for 3 min after a session opens or the student comes back (so its 20–30 s cold start overlaps the camera starting or the phone pairing) and never while its own socket is live, Glimmer only when it is the brain; one ping per service is in flight across all sessions, so a cold start never spawns a second container. The start page wakes voice + SAM 3.1 once on the first touch of a choice (rate-limited per IP and to one per 30 s overall). Measured 2026-10-31 (w2 copy, idle set to 1 min, still fake camera): rest after 63 s, tracking socket closed 0.3 s later; after the last use SAM 3.1 was gone ~2 min later and voice / SAM ~3 min later (deployed windows then 60 s and 120 s; Modal adds ~1 min to each window).
- **Public-link guards** (`lens/limits.py`, all `LENS_*` in `.env.example`): 8 tutor sessions and 4 tracking sessions at once, 40 session starts per IP per hour, 6 checks and 20 spoken turns per session per minute, 4000 Spark calls and $15 estimated Spark spend per UTC day; each shows the student a friendly message (WebSocket close 4429 for refusals). The access code becomes a signed cookie; the pairing QR carries a 15-minute pairing token, never the code. A stalled Spark call never leaves the session silent: a hedged image read gives up after 150 s (a stream with no byte for 75 s is retried), a check shows and says "taking longer than usual" after 20 s and is dropped after 170 s, a spoken reply says "One moment." after 6 s and apologises after 30 s. No automatic switch to Glimmer: switched manually via configuration (README, *Switching to self-hosted Muse Glimmer*). Each tutor session logs `session cost (estimate)` (Spark tokens and cost + GPU seconds it kept warm) when it ends.

- **Voice transport:** hand-built, no LiveKit. Speech recognition can garble accented English and maths words; the tutor and the notes are told so and read the transcript for what was most likely meant (live, 2026-11-03: "why equals forty five plus twenty tea" was taken as y = 45 + 20t). Browser VAD (loudness + a voicing test, so taps, clicks and rustle are not speech) cuts utterances; speech over Lens first only lowers its voice, and Lens stops for real after ~0.3 s of voiced speech (about 0.4 s in) or when the server's transcript has words; a turn with no words (or Lens's own echo) never stops Lens or drops the turn before it; speech is streamed up over the `/ws/tutor` WebSocket in 200 ms PCM chunks while the student talks (the page frame goes when they start), the server transcribes at the first 200 ms of quiet, and the turn ends after 400 ms of silence; speech that resumes within 1.2 s is merged back into the same turn, so a pause mid-sentence does not cut the student off. Omnilingual ASR → Spark (`minimal`, streamed) → Kokoro (first piece may end at a clause boundary) → PCM back in 0.4 s slices. One less service to run. Measured 2026-10-30 from Ethiopia against Modal us-east, warm, page already read: end of speech → first audio at the client ≈ 1.8 s (0.4 s silence wait + 1.4 s median), down from ≈ 3.3 s (0.7 s + 2.6 s; the frame and the WAV were uploaded only after the student stopped). Server side: transcript 0.11 s, first model text 0.94 s, first audio 1.11 s after the turn ended. An instant "Mm." acknowledgement was not needed.
- **SAM 3.1 tracks live; SAM 3 stays for still frames.** Live tracking runs `facebook/sam3.1` (`sam3.1_multiplex.pt`) in Meta's own `sam3` package on Modal (`modal_apps/sam31.py`, app `muna-lens-sam31`, one L40S). The package is built for whole videos; Lens feeds it a live stream one frame at a time (no look-ahead: the detector's prefetch would otherwise run on the previous frame) with one concept session for "hand" and one for "pen", sharing one image encoding per frame. The browser sends 512 px JPEGs (~15 kB) offered at 5 fps with at most 2 unanswered, over `/ws/track` (the app relays, so the endpoint and its token stay server-side). From the tracks, `lens/tracking.py` derives the phase (writing / paused / clear), the pointer (pen tip, else the fingertip reaching farthest in) and `line_done` (a writing spell ended and the hand left the region the pen tip covered), which triggers the page check. SAM 3 found no handwriting concept ("handwriting", "handwritten text", "equation", "text": 0 masks on real pages), so the outline follows the page by template matching the mistake step (plumbing, not a model): `lens/follow.py`, run in the app's tracking relay on the frames it forwards, started from the very frame the check read (the tutor hands it to the relay by track id), matching the ink (dark strokes minus the local paper, softened) over shift, turn (±8°) and scale; the outline fades while the step is covered and is hidden when it cannot be found or looks like another place. SAM 3 treats a box as an example and returns every similar line, so the outline mask is the returned mask lying inside the step box (`pointing.step_mask`), else the box. The still-frame service (`muna-lens-sam`, SAM 3 via Transformers) still draws the outline mask and answers "this line?" when tracking is off; the pixel detector stays the trigger fallback.
- **Accessibility** (deaf / hard-of-hearing, speech-impaired, low-vision students): one settings panel on the Lens screen (top bar, keyboard reachable, remembered per browser). Live captions of what Lens says and heard are **on by default** (they cost nothing and a student who cannot hear Lens should not have to find a setting first). Type-to-talk takes the spoken reply path (same pointing, look frame, answer guard), no ASR. Voice on/off (off = captions only; replies still come as text) and Kokoro speaking speed 0.7–1.4. "Read my page aloud" (button or asked in words) reads the steps already read, said in words by `lens/speakmath.py` (no model, so it can only say what the student wrote; a flagged step is named as the outlined one; Lens's own framing has no digits). "End session" shows a plain-words summary (what they worked on, steps checked, mistakes and whether fixed, no answers) with a text download; nothing is stored server-side, no accounts, no memory across sessions. Protocol: `text`, `settings`, `read_page`, `end` → `summary` in `lens/tutor.py`.
- **Stack:** Vite + React + TypeScript frontend, FastAPI backend (Python, uv). Lens is a client-only camera app, so Vite fits better than Next.js; FastAPI serves the built frontend, so a deploy is one process. `make dev` runs both locally.

## Shared contract

Every lane codes against this shape so lanes can build in parallel. Change it in one place: here.

```
Page {
  problem: string | null          # what is being solved, if known
  steps: Step[]                   # in reading order
}
Step {
  id: string
  latex: string                   # transcription
  bbox: [x, y, w, h]              # normalised 0–1 on the frame
  status: "unchecked" | "ok" | "error" | "unverifiable"
  verified_by: "sympy" | "model" | null
}
Finding {
  step_id: string                 # first erroneous step
  kind: string                    # e.g. "sign error moving a term across ="
  mask: polygon | null            # from SAM
  hint: string                    # Socratic, answer-free
}
```

Rules:
- External datasets are **downloaded at run time** into `.cache/eval/` (gitignored), never committed. The repo carries only the loaders and harness.
- Own-set pages are committed only with each writer's consent; otherwise they live in the gitignored cache too.
- Report scores **per source**; a FERMAT score is not a camera-photo score.
- FERMAT and the own set measure error finding; EDU-CIRCUIT-HW measures reading only.

## Build order (DAG)

Each node lists what it depends on and its **done** criterion. Nodes in different lanes with satisfied dependencies are built in parallel. Every node ships with a test that proves its done criterion.

```
A0 ─┬─ A1 ─┬─ A2 ── A3 ──┬── A5 ── A4 ──┐
    │      │             │              │
    │      │      E0 ────┘ (A5 = eval)  │
    │      │                            ├── F1 ── F2 ── F3
    ├─ C1 ─┴─ C2 ───────────────────────┤
    │                                   │
    ├─ D1 ── D2 ────────────────────────┤
    │                                   │
    └─ V1 ── V2 ────────────────────────┘
```

### Lane 0: foundation
- **A0 Repo skeleton.** `.gitignore` (covers `.env`), `.env.example`, frontend + backend scaffolds, one command to run locally. *Done:* app boots locally; `git status` shows no secret files.

### Lane A: brain (Muse Spark Open)
- **A1 Spark client** (A0). Text, image and audio inputs, streaming, `reasoning_effort` per call, retries, token/cost logging per call. *Done:* one call of each input type passes a test and logs its cost.
- **A2 Page reader** (A1). Frame → `Page` (steps in order, LaTeX, bbox, problem if visible). *Done:* on 10 eval pages, every step is transcribed and boxed in the right order.
- **A3 Step checker** (A2). For each consecutive pair, SymPy checks the step follows; non-algebraic steps go to Spark and are marked `verified_by: "model"`. Output: first erroneous step. *Done:* passes unit tests with planted algebra, calculus and sign errors, and marks correct derivations all `ok`.
- **E0 Eval sets** (no deps, starts day 1, runs alongside everything). Three sources, each covering a gap the others leave (see *Evaluation data* below). *Done:* a loader yields every item from all three as `(image, problem, first_error_step | "none")`, and the own set has its first 10 pages.
- **A5 Eval harness** (A3, E0). Runs reader + checker over the eval sets; reports caught / missed / false alarms and cost per page, separately per source. *Done:* one command prints the scorecard. **Gate:** false alarms ≤ 1 in 20 correct pages before A4 work is tuned further; the scorecard is a demo asset.
- **A4 Hint writer + guardrail** (A3). Writes one Socratic hint for a `Finding`; a second check rejects any hint that contains the corrected step, the result or an equivalent expression (SymPy compare against the expected next line), then regenerates. *Done:* across the eval errors, zero hints leak the answer.

### Lane C: SAM 3.1
- **C1 SAM service on Modal** (A0). Endpoint: image + (box or noun-phrase prompt) → masks. Warm-keeping switch for sessions. *Done:* returns a mask for a step bbox and for "fingertip" on test photos; warm latency measured and logged.
- **C2 Pointing + outlines** (C1, A2). Fingertip/pen-tip location → nearest `Step`; step bbox → clean outline mask for the `Finding`. *Done:* on 10 photos of a finger on a line, the right step is picked ≥ 9 times.

### Lane D: capture + screen
- **D1 Capture client** (A0). Webcam mode and phone mode (QR pairing; phone browser streams frames; screen wake lock). Change detection: send a frame only when new ink appears and the hand is clear (fallback once tracking lands). *Done:* phone pairs by QR in under 10 s; writing a new line triggers exactly one frame upload.
- **D2 Lens screen** (D1, contract). Live page, outline from `Finding.mask`, tutor pointer, steps panel (ok / error). Fresh design. *Done:* renders a recorded `Page` + `Finding` correctly on laptop and phone widths.

### Lane V: voice
- **V1 ASR + TTS services** (A0). Omnilingual ASR and Kokoro on Modal. *Done:* a 5 s utterance transcribes and a sentence synthesises; both latencies logged.
- **V2 Voice loop** (V1, A1; custom WebSocket voice loop with VAD, interruption, and streamed ASR/TTS). Voice activity detection, interruption (student talks → tutor stops), ASR → Spark (`minimal` effort) → TTS. *Done:* spoken question to first audio reply under ~2.5 s on a warm system; barge-in stops speech.

### Lane F: integration (after lanes converge)
- **F1 Tutor agent** (A5, A4, C2, D2, V2). Proactive opening (asks what they are working on), page-change → read → check → if a verified `Finding`, outline it and speak the hint; answers "this line?" questions using pointing. *Done:* a full session on a real problem: the student writes a wrong line, Lens outlines it and asks a question; the student fixes it and Lens moves on, without leaking the answer.
- **F2 Deploy** (F1). Production deploy on Modal (`make deploy-web`) + GPU services; access control configured. *Done:* an outside tester completes F1's session from their own device via the URL.
- **F3 Real-student testing + cost** (F2). Several real students, own homework; measure cost per hour and error-catch rate in the wild; record consented clips. *Done:* cost/hour and scorecard numbers written down; at least one usable clip.
